HelixScreen after 1.0: ten ideas

Brainstorm, 2026-09-30. Sketches and rationale, not a plan. Nothing here is scoped, scheduled or committed to.

The filter. HelixScreen's edge over a web UI is that the person is standing at the machine, with a touchscreen, a camera and (often) an accelerometer. Each idea below is something that would be clumsy or pointless in a browser, and each leans on a subsystem that already exists.

Already shipped, so not on this list: the live g-code viewer with layer-synced progress and tap-to-exclude. Tool-path-level granularity would be a refinement of that, not a new headliner.

About the mockups. They show the idea, at roughly 480×288. They are not layout specs, token-accurate, or aware of breakpoints. Names of existing subsystems come from a survey of the repo and should be re-verified before anyone builds on them.

1

First-Layer Coach

calibrationcamera

The highest-anxiety minutes of any print, with the tools in one place.

What it is

During layer 1 the print-status screen swaps to a coaching layout: a magnified camera crop that follows the nozzle, a three-card "too high / right / too low" reference, and the existing live Z adjustment. You nudge Z and watch the line width change on camera.

Why it's good

Nobody wants to kneel at the printer with a flashlight. The pieces (camera, babystep, Z-offset flow) all exist; the novelty is putting them in one frame.

Builds on

camera_stream, calibration_zoffset live adjust, print-status panel.

Open questions

  • How does the crop follow the nozzle? Fixed per-printer region the user sets once, vs. mapping toolhead XY to image coordinates.
  • Printers with no camera: degrade to the gallery + dial only, or skip the feature?
  • Is the "squish" gallery photos, drawings, or both? Photos age better than drawings.
  • Optional later: auto-judge squish from the frame. Probably out of scope at first.

Wow: dial in a perfect first layer without squinting.

2

Color Studio

multi-material3D viewer

Cash in the AMS investment: assign lanes by touching the model.

What it is

When a multi-material job is chosen, the g-code viewer renders the model colored by tool. Tapping a part selects its color; tapping a lane assigns it. Mismatches (temperature gap, material family, too little filament) show inline, plus a rough purge and time cost.

Why it's good

Today the mapping step is a list. Touching the red part to say "this goes in lane 3" is how a person thinks about it, and the screen can warn before a bad print, not after.

Builds on

AMS lane state / slot_registry, the filament mapping modal, the g-code viewer, the filament catalog.

Open questions

  • The g-code pipeline already keeps a tools-used cache (docs/devel/architecture/16-gcode-pipeline.md). Check whether it carries per-tool geometry, or only which tools appear. Coloring by tool needs the former.
  • Purge estimate is backend-specific (wipe tower vs. poop chute vs. toolchanger). Start with "unknown" on backends that can't say.
  • Stay vendor-neutral per the vendor-abstraction rule: ask capability questions, not AFC/HH/ACE questions.

Wow: "this red goes in lane 3" by literally touching the red part.

3

Machine Health

accelerometermaintenance

The printer's acoustic fingerprint, and a garage for its wear parts.

What it is

Take a baseline resonance capture after a good tuning, then re-sample on a schedule (or opportunistically, e.g. after a print). Drift in frequency or amplitude flags loose belts, worn bearings, or a mounting problem before it shows up as ringing. Alongside, a "Garage" tracks wear-part hours and offers guided service steps when they're due.

Why it's good

It turns the accelerometer from a setup tool into an ongoing one. No web UI can do this at the machine, and the DSP and charts are already built.

Builds on

Input shaper and belt-tension panels (docs/devel/INPUT_SHAPER.md, BELT_TENSION.md), detection_manager, notifications, print history for hour counts.

Open questions

  • The current foundation is unproven. BELT_TUNER.md states that the tuner has never measured a real belt and its thresholds are circular. Drift detection needs real-hardware data first, so this likely starts as a data-collection spike.
  • Signal quality: can we separate "belt drifted" from "different temperature / different day" without false alarms? This needs real data before promising anything.
  • Who owns the scan? Klipper resonance tests move the toolhead, so they can only run idle. Schedule it, don't surprise people.
  • Garage is independent of the DSP half and could ship alone as a simple hour counter.

Wow: "Belt B has drifted since last week. Tension it?"

4

Tuning Dial

in-print UX

One thumb, one control, everything you'd tweak mid-print.

What it is

A single radial surface in the print-status tune path. Each arc is a live parameter (speed, flow, pressure advance, fan, Z babystep). Tap an arc to select it, drag around the ring to change it, and see the printer's actual response beside it.

Why it's good

Touchscreens usually destroy the feel of a knob. A well-tuned radial control gets some of it back and replaces a stack of sliders and +/- buttons.

Builds on

The existing tune controls and subject bindings, docs/devel/GESTURE_RECOGNITION.md for the drag handling.

Open questions

  • Is the ring better than sliders at all, or only more photogenic? Needs a hands-on prototype and a real thumb.
  • Undo / "revert to slicer values" must be one tap, because dragging is easy to overshoot.
  • Pressure advance has a different failure mode than flow. It may want a confirm step or a narrower range.

Wow: nudging flow with your thumb like a mixing board.

5

Spool Station

inventorylabels

Scanner + Spoolman + label printer, welded into one loop.

What it is

Scan a new spool's barcode, pre-fill a Spoolman entry, print a QR label, stick it on. Later, scanning that label at a lane (or at the printer) loads the spool into the right slot with the right settings. It is almost entirely existing parts: a scanner, four label-printer protocols, and Spoolman CRUD.

Why it's good

It makes the printer the natural place to manage filament. It is also very demoable: scan, beep, label prints, done.

Builds on

snapshot_qr_scanner / barcode scanner settings, the label-printer system (docs/devel/LABEL_PRINTER_SYSTEM.md), spoolman_manager, slot_registry.

Open questions

  • Barcode to product lookup needs a data source. Manufacturers' barcodes are not consistently public, so the fallback of "scan, then type three fields" must be good.
  • Offline first: a lookup service would be the only network dependency and should be optional.
  • Label content and size per printer model; probably a couple of good defaults.

Wow: the printer becomes a filament inventory station.

6

Rescue Mode

recoverydetection

Detection says "something is wrong." Rescue says "here's how to save it."

What it is

When spaghetti, clog or runout detection fires, offer a guided flow instead of a dismissible alert: park safely, walk through clearing the jam with clear steps, re-prime, then choose the layer to resume from. The layer picker sits on the viewer the app already has.

Why it's good

It's the difference between a notification and a rescued 12-hour print. Of the ten, this has the biggest emotional payoff.

Builds on

detection_manager, recovery_service, the power-loss-recovery offer, camera snapshots, the live layer viewer.

Open questions

  • Resume-from-layer is the hard part. It needs rewritten g-code (restore temps, positions, extruder state, fan, and Z) and is easy to get subtly wrong. POWER_LOSS_RECOVERY.md documents a mandatory probe-before-resume safety invariant that any operator-triggered resume should inherit.
  • Resuming into a part that has cooled and shrunk is not always salvageable. The UI should be honest: "likely to show a seam."
  • Runout/clog steps are hardware-specific (AMS vs. direct drive); keep step content data-driven.

Wow: salvage a long print instead of scraping it off the bed.

7

Showpiece Mode

delightsound + LED

Make the printer look, sound and feel like a product.

What it is

A print-time ambient mode. The screen becomes a poster (model, progress ring, time left), the LEDs breathe with progress, and finishing a print plays a custom fanfare composed on the chiptune engine. An optional generated soundtrack could evolve with layer height.

Why it's good

The synthesizer, LED backends and screensaver framework exist and are uncoordinated. A small amount of choreography makes a printer on a shelf feel like an object of desire, and gives people a reason to show their screen off.

Builds on

Sound synthesizer/tracker modules (docs/devel/SOUND_SYSTEM.md), LED control, screensavers (docs/devel/SCREENSAVERS.md), print-lifecycle state.

Open questions

  • Burn-in and power: an always-on poster is not a screensaver. Dimming policy matters on cheap panels.
  • Sound is polarizing in a shared room. Quiet hours and an easy mute are required, not optional.
  • Generative soundtrack is the speculative part; the fanfare and LED sync are cheap wins on their own.
  • Could share a theme pack format so people swap "looks" (screen + LED + sound) as one unit.

Wow: finishing a print feels like a win.

8

Cost & Energy

historyroadmap gap

What did that print actually cost?

What it is

Combine filament used (already tracked) with energy from a metered smart plug and a couple of user-entered prices, and show a per-print cost on the completion screen and in history, plus monthly totals.

Why it's good

Power consumption display is an open roadmap backlog item, and people love knowing what a hobby costs. It makes the history dashboard something you check on purpose.

Builds on

power_device_state, the filament consumption tracker, the history dashboard, Spoolman cost-per-kg if present.

Open questions

  • Where does energy data come from? Not every Moonraker power device reports watts or kWh. This is the main risk; check what Moonraker actually exposes per device type before designing around it.
  • Attribution: a plug powers the whole printer, including idle and preheat. Decide whether "cost of a print" includes warm-up.
  • Without a metered plug it can still ship as filament-only cost, and power becomes a bonus.

Wow: a real per-print receipt, at the machine.

9

Automations

platformplugins

When this happens, do that. No config file required.

What it is

A small on-device rules engine. Triggers are printer events and state thresholds; actions are macros, power, LED, fan, sound and notifications. Rules are built by picking chips on the touchscreen, with no file editing.

Why it's good

Plugin ecosystem expansion is the stated roadmap priority. An automation layer gives plugins a natural surface (contribute a trigger or an action), and gives power users their own behaviors without touching config.

Builds on

The macro executor, the subject/event system, notifications, power/LED/fan state, the plugin system.

Open questions

  • Safety. Rules that move the machine or change heaters need guard rails: no motion while printing unless explicit, loop detection (A triggers B triggers A), and a visible "last fired" log.
  • Persistence and migration: rules are user data, so they belong in the versioned config with a schema from day one.
  • Keep the vocabulary small at launch: maybe 8 triggers and 8 actions, done well.
  • Klipper already has delayed_gcode and macros. Automations should complement, not reinvent, them.

Wow: "when the print finishes, park and turn the lights off," built in ten seconds.

10

Material IQ

filamentdryer

Change the filament and every setting follows it.

What it is

The active material becomes the source of truth. Loading a filament applies its temps, retraction and pressure advance; combinations that shouldn't share a path or purge temperature are flagged; and per-lane drying schedules are managed alongside.

Why it's good

Failed first layers from a stale profile are one of the most common, most avoidable failures. A visible diff keeps it trustworthy instead of magic.

Builds on

Filament catalog and variants, active_material_provider, slot overrides, the AMS backends, dryer control (docs/devel/BED_DRYING.md, FILAMENT_ENVIRONMENT_ZONES.md). Per-lane drying is an existing deferred item.

Open questions

  • Conflict with the slicer. Slicer g-code already sets temps. Decide what this owns: probably manual loads, pre-print checks and things the slicer can't know (PA, dry state), not overriding a sliced job.
  • Per-spool overrides stop the feature from fighting a user who tuned their own PA. They need a clear "reset to catalog" path.
  • Drying already has a model: BED_DRYING.md and FILAMENT_ENVIRONMENT_ZONES.md (drying-state folding, concurrency cap). Schedules should extend that, not sit beside it.
  • Per-lane drying is hardware-dependent; ACE is the one with dryer control today, so the scope should follow capabilities.

Wow: swap PETG for PLA and every setting is already right.

Cross-cutting notes

  • Stories they tell together: see it and nail it (1, 4), feed it (2, 5, 10), keep it healthy (3, 6), show it off and account for it (7, 8), and make it yours (9).
  • Natural pairs: 2 and 10 both want a richer "what filament is on which lane" model; 5 feeds 10; 9 is the hook that lets 6, 7 and 8 become user-configurable later.
  • Hardest to de-risk: 6 (resume g-code), 3 (signal quality), 8 (energy data source). Each deserves a small spike before anyone promises a date.
  • Cheapest big smiles: 1, 4 and the fanfare half of 7 reuse almost everything already shipped.
  • Conventions to respect: declarative UI (data in C++, appearance in XML), vendor abstraction (capability questions, not vendor names), and the firmware build's no-exceptions/no-RTTI rules for anything shared with ESP32.
