The part that actually needs deciding is what a tap does. This is a touchscreen bolted to a printer, often at waist height, frequently brushed past. A tap that commits motion immediately is the difference between a great feature and a toolhead that wanders off because someone leaned on the panel.

So: how does a tap commit?

A
Tap moves immediately
0,0
Tap anywhere → moving now

One gesture, nothing in the way. Feels immediate and it is how a phone map behaves.
Also means any stray contact is a real move. No undo on a gantry.

B
Tap aims, Go commits
0,0
→ X 55.0 Y 62.0 Go

Tap drops a target and shows the exact coordinates it resolved to. Nothing moves until Go.
Drag the target to fine-tune, and you can read the number before you commit. Two gestures instead of one.

Three things this tab needs regardless of which you pick:
• Unhomed: the map is meaningless without a reference, so it greys out with a Home prompt rather than guessing.
• Printing: disabled outright, same as the rest of the panel already does via nav_buttons_enabled.
• Z stays where it is. A tap moves XY only, at whatever Z you are at. If you are at Z 0.2 that is a nozzle dragged across the bed, so the map should require a minimum Z or offer to lift first. Worth its own decision later.
