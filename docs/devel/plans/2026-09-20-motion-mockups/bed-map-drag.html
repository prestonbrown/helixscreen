The problem with tap-only

Your finger is bigger than the precision you want. On a 480px panel this map gets maybe 170px across. A 220mm bed at 170px is about 1.3mm per pixel, and a fingertip covers 20–30 pixels. So at the moment you commit, your finger is hiding roughly 30mm of bed and you cannot see where you are about to send the nozzle. Tap-only is fine for "go over there". It cannot do "go to that spot".

So what should drag do?

Tap stays immediate either way, as you picked. This is only about what happens when the finger moves.

A
Drag aims · release commits
0,0
target X 70.4  Y 44.9  ·  lift to go

The target rides above your fingertip so you can see it, with a live coordinate readout. Nothing moves until you lift.
Solves the occlusion problem completely, and it is one move per gesture. Drag off the bed edge and it clamps to the axis limit instead of erroring.

B
Drag live-follows
0,0
head chasing · lagging behind finger

The head chases your finger as you drag. Your coalescer makes this safe: one move in flight, latest target replaces the pending one, so at most one move outlives your release.
But it will not feel smooth. Every queued move decelerates to zero at its target, so the head hops rather than glides, and your finger still hides the destination.

I'd take A, and it composes with what you already chose. Tap is the quick gesture, "go over there now". Drag is the precise one, "go exactly there", with the number visible before you commit. B is not unsafe thanks to the coalescer, it just spends a lot of gantry motion delivering a worse result than A, and it still cannot show you where you are aiming.
